Key Diagnostic Test Parameters

The optical sensor evaluates colorimetric changes across several critical biological indicators:

  • Glucose: Measured in concentration levels such as 100 mg/dL (or 100 mg·dL-1). Standard reference detection begins near 50 mg/dL100.
  • Leukocytes (WBC): Indicates immune response and presence of White Blood Cells in urine.
  • Erythrocytes (RBC): Detects intact Red Blood Cells or free hemoglobin associated with renal bleeding.
  • Acidity (pH): Measures hydrogen ion concentrations, typically ranging from 5.0 to 8.5 pH.
  • Specific Gravity (SG): Assesses hydration status and kidney concentrating ability.
  • Nitrite: Screened as a primary biological marker for bacterial UTI presence.

Step-by-Step Diagnostic Process

  1. Sample Collection: Collect a fresh, midstream urine sample in a clean, dry container.
  2. Strip Immersion: Dip the test strip into the sample for exactly one second, ensuring all reagent pads are fully submerged.
  3. Excess Liquid Removal: Touch the edge of the strip against the rim of the vessel to remove excess urine.
  4. Optical Reader Placement: Insert the strip into the tray alignment slot. The digital reader scans the pads under controlled LED light.
  5. Automated Analysis: Results are displayed instantly on-screen and automatically transmitted to the Electronic Health Record (EHR).
