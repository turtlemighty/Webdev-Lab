Urine Testing Reference & Stakeholder Guide

Standard Operating Procedures, Tested Analytes, Roles, and Terminology

Standard Testing Procedure

  1. Sample Collection: Obtain a fresh, midstream urine specimen in a sterile container.
  2. Strip Preparation: Remove a reagent test strip from the sealed bottle, avoiding touching the chemical pads.
  3. Immersion: Dip all reagent pads completely into the urine sample for exactly one second.
  4. Excess Removal: Wipe the edge of the strip against the rim of the container to remove surplus fluid.
  5. Optical Scanning: Place the strip into the digital optical reader or wait the specified time for visual evaluation.
  6. Results Recording: Log analyte values immediately into the Electronic Health Record (EHR).

Target Urine Analytes

  • Glucose
  • Protein
  • Ketones
  • Blood
  • Bilirubin
  • Nitrite
  • Leukocytes
  • pH
  • Specific Gravity
  • Urobilinogen

Healthcare Stakeholders & Roles

  • Doctor
    • Orders diagnostic tests based on patient symptom profiles.
    • Interprets clinical analyte data to establish diagnoses and treatment plans.
  • Laboratory Technician
    • Executes quality control checks and calibrates optical analyzers.
    • Processes urine samples and ensures accurate result transmission.
  • Patient
    • Provides midstream urine specimens following sanitary collection instructions.
    • Reviews diagnostic findings with their clinical care team.
  • Hospital Administrator
    • Manages clinical analyzer procurement and reagent inventory.
    • Ensures laboratory operations comply with healthcare regulatory standards.

Medical Abbreviations Reference

EHR
Electronic Health Record: A digital version of a patient's medical history and real-time diagnostic results.
UTI
Urinary Tract Infection: An infection in any part of the urinary system, commonly screened via nitrites and leukocytes.
WBC
White Blood Cells: Immune system cells whose presence in urine (leukocyte esterase) indicates inflammation or infection.
RBC
Red Blood Cells: Erythrocytes evaluated to detect micro-bleeding or renal injury.
SG
Specific Gravity: The ratio of urine density compared to water, measuring kidney concentrating ability and hydration status.
pH
Potential of Hydrogen: The logarithmic scale indicating acidity or alkalinity of the urine sample.
